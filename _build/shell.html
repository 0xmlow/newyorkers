TEN STYLES. ONE CITY.Ten artists. Forty two rooms. For each one, the real moment they had in the city, a part of the city wrapped in their art overnight, and the show: a real New York exhibition of theirs rebuilt as a hall that hangs MLow's New Yorkers restyled in their hand, with a vault of every one. One trick in every room you can only get by walking there, the best ideas real exhibitions ever had for these artists rebuilt inside the shows, an audio guide, and a finale where four of them built the rides. Everybody here has an eye flower for a head.

Every artist now ends in an Impossible Room, something no building could do: dots that cover you if you stand still, an x-ray in the middle of your eye, a floor you draw on by walking, the Great Wave you stand beside, noises you click into the air, a corridor that stretches as you walk, a camera that screen tests you in four inks, a sky that swirls round you, a canvas painted live by four cameras circling a marble head, and stencils that only exist in the corner of your eye. It starts at THE STYLE MACHINE: pick one of 219 New Yorkers, press an artist and watch it change hands, all ten at once on the wall behind you, and hang your own wall. Then the shows: Kusama's thousand boats, Basquiat's first New York solo at Annina Nosei, the Pop Shop, thirty six views unrolled, a Lichtenstein Interior, Dali at MoMA in 1941, Warhol's cows and clouds at Castelli, Van Gogh at MoMA in 1935, Picasso's forty years in 1939, and a thrift shop where nothing stays priced. Every piece opens with the census portrait it came from beside it. Before that: Kusama on Wall Street in 1968 and Chelsea in dots. SAMO on the SoHo doors and Great Jones Street in crowns. Haring's chalk on the subway's black paper and Harlem outlined. Hokusai under low light at the Met and the Battery during Sandy as a woodblock. Lichtenstein's first Castelli show and Times Square inked. Dali's bathtub through a Fifth Avenue window and the 1939 World's Fair melting. The Silver Factory and Empire. The crowd of phones at The Starry Night and DUMBO under his sky. The Picasso curtain at the Four Seasons and Rockefeller cubed. Sixty dollar canvases by Central Park and the Meatpacking District after dark.

THAT IS THE CITY

TEN STYLES. ONE CITY.

42 rooms, ten artists, one city, ten shows and a fairground, built by NEW YORKERS by MLow with art direction and facts checked by GPT-6 Astra on FLORA. The moments are real: Anatomic Explosion on Wall Street in 1968, SAMO in SoHo, the subway chalk drawings and Crack is Wack, Lichtenstein at Castelli in February 1962, Dali and the Bonwit Teller window in March 1939, the Silver Factory and Empire in 1964, The Starry Night at MoMA since 1941, Le Tricorne at the Four Seasons from 1959 to 2014, the sixty dollar stall on October 12, 2013 and Sirens of the Lambs. The wraps are what the city would look like if they had painted it overnight. The shows are real exhibitions, rebuilt to hang the New Yorkers: every one of the 2,207 restyled pieces is in a vault, and every piece on a wall opens beside the census portrait it came from.
