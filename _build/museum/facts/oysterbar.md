## The real plan
On the lower level of Grand Central, reached by the Oyster Bar ramps down from the Main Concourse. At the foot of the ramps, under the pedestrian bridge between Vanderbilt Hall and the Main Concourse, the Guastavino tiled arches form the whispering gallery. The restaurant is in three parts: the main dining room under a dozen low Guastavino tile vaults on piers (one tile type throughout), the counters with individual stools, and the wood panelled Saloon with a long bar. Opened 1913 as the Grand Central Terminal Restaurant; interior landmark 23 September 1980 (with the terminal); fire in 1997; employee owned since 1999.
