## Real plan
Grand Army Plaza is a set of concentric ovals at the north corner of Prospect Park. The Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch (John H. Duncan, 1889 to 1892) is 80 ft high, 80 x 50 ft in plan, opening 50 ft high and 35 ft wide, granite, with MacMonnies' quadriga (1898) on top, his Army and Navy groups on the south pedestals (1901) and Eakins and O'Donovan's Lincoln and Grant reliefs inside the opening; the inscription runs on the attic of the south facade. Bailey Fountain (1932) stands in the plaza. Eastern Parkway (Olmsted and Vaux, 1870 to 1874) runs east from the plaza: six lane main road, malls planted with elms, service roads with maples. The Central Library and the Brooklyn Museum are both on its south side.

## FIXED
- Arch cut down to the real 80 ft: piers, attic, cornice, quadriga and inscription lowered 6.5 m, opening 15.2 m high.
- Brooklyn Museum moved to the south side of the parkway (x -60, z 110), its colonnade now facing north onto the parkway. Its four colonnade mounts moved with it (same count, same size, now facing the parkway); audit shows them reachable and not backwards.
- Mall trees set to species elm.
- Six eggs: easternparkway-arch, easternparkway-reliefs, easternparkway-quadriga, easternparkway-first, easternparkway-subway, easternparkway-fountain.

## LEFT
- Arch depth (8 m vs 15 m) and its orientation: both would move the six arch mounts and the census wall.
- The plaza's real geometry: the arch at the park end of the oval with Flatbush Avenue around it and Bailey Fountain in the middle. A restructure.
- The museum is compressed close to the plaza; really it is at Washington Avenue, about 600 m east.
- Audit: 8 backwards (rescued) mounts on the arch's inner faces and the mall screens; none of them were touched.
