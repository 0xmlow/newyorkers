## The real plan
The former mail sorting room of the Farley Post Office (McKim, Mead & White, opened 1914). Three riveted steel trusses, each 150 ft long and 50 ft apart, each a pair of bents about 3 ft apart. The roof is four catenary glass vaults rising 92 ft. Train hall courtyard about 230 by 157 ft. Opened January 1, 2021, connects to 17 of Penn Station's 21 tracks. The clock (Peter Pennoyer Architects) is 12 ft tall, about 6 ft wide, four sided, pewter like finish, hung from the centre truss about 25 ft up. Art: Elmgreen & Dragset, The Hive (31st Street entry ceiling); Kehinde Wiley, Go (33rd Street entry ceiling, stained glass); Stan Douglas, Penn Station's Half Century (nine photographs, ticketed waiting room).

## FIXED
- Clock rebuilt as a tall pewter case (about 1.8 by 3.6 m plus stepped caps), hung from the bottom chord of the centre truss, bottom about 7.2 m up, smaller opal faces on all four sides.
- Added a mirrored upside down cluster of buildings over the left end of the entrance, standing for The Hive.
- Added a glowing ceiling panel over the right end of the entrance, standing for Wiley's Go (colour field only, no figures).
- Removed the arrow glyph from the TRACKS signs.
- Six easter eggs: penn-trusses, penn-blackout, penn-clock, penn-hive, penn-go, penn-motto.
