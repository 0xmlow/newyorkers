# unisphere: the Unisphere, Flushing Meadows Corona Park

## Sources
- Unisphere, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unisphere

## The real plan
Built for the 1964 World's Fair by U.S. Steel (American Bridge Company), designed by Gilmore D. Clarke. Type 304L stainless steel globe 120 ft across, 140 ft tall overall, on a 20 ft inverted tripod. Tilted 23.5 degrees. Over 500 steel landmass pieces, three orbit rings on aircraft cable. Reflecting pool 310 ft across with 48 pairs of fountainheads round its outer edge. Walks radiate north, northeast, southeast and south; the Queens Museum is to the west, the tennis center to the north, the New York State Pavilion to the south.

## FIXED
- Globe, meridians, parallels and continents now tilted 23.5 degrees about the globe centre.
- Fountain arcs from 36 to 48.
- Walks re-aimed to north, northeast, southeast and south (north is minus z), allees follow them.
- Pavilion towers and the Tent of Tomorrow moved from the northeast to the south.
- Six eggs: unisphere-tilt, unisphere-orbits, unisphere-kahnawake, unisphere-sri-lanka, unisphere-perisphere-piles, unisphere-antarctica-grass.

## LEFT
- Scale: the real pool is about 2.7 times wider than the room's basin and the visitor would walk outside it. The circular route and all 24 mounts sit at fixed radii, so resizing means rehanging.
- Globe diameter kept at 22 m, because the basin rim mounts at radius 19 would sit inside a 37 m globe.
- Landmasses are eight blobs, not the 500 plus pieces; capital lights not modelled.
- Queens Museum to the west and the tennis center to the north are not modelled.
- I did not check which continent blob sits where on the globe, so the clue for the Kahnawake and Sri Lanka eggs points at the nearest large mass.
