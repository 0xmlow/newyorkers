## The real plan
Wooden track coaster opened 26 June 1927 at Surf Avenue and West 10th Street, designed by Vernon Keenan. Triple out and back, 2,640 ft of track, lift hill 75 ft (85 ft to the top of the sign), first drop 58.1 degrees, 60 mph, twelve drops and six fan turns. Brown wooden track with red wooden fencing on a white steel frame (I beams, tie bars, diagonal bracing, riveted plates). Red CYCLONE letters on the east and west sides of the lift hill, a 45 ft vertical CYCLONE sign at the station. Trains of three cars, four rows of two, 24 riders. The Wonder Wheel (1920, 150 ft, 24 cars: 8 white fixed, 16 swinging red/yellow or blue/yellow, blue legs) is on West 12th Street, west of the Cyclone; the Parachute Jump is further west.

## FIXED
- Lift hill lowered to 75 ft; lift speed rule now keyed to the lift segment, so the train still climbs slowly.
- Four seat rows per car.
- Red CYCLONE letters on both sides of the lift crest.
- Wonder Wheel moved west of the coaster, 24 cars with the real colour scheme, blue legs.
- Queue fence panel works turned to face the queue (backwards 6 to 0).
- Six eggs: cyclone-crest, cyclone-steel, cyclone-franco, cyclone-lindbergh, cyclone-quarter, cyclone-wonderwheel.

## LEFT
- Track layout is a simple loop, not the real triple out and back with six fan turns and twelve drops: a full spline rebuild.
- Red wooden fencing along the track and the brown track colour: needs a rail material pass along the spline.
- The real Cyclone sits a block inland on Surf Avenue, not on the boardwalk: moving the boardwalk would strand the six billboard works.
- The 45 ft vertical station sign: not added, the station roof is too low in this model.
