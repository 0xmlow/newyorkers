## The real plan
Croton Aqueduct bridge, water first flowed May 30, 1848, walkway finished 1864. 1,450 ft long, deck about 140 ft above the Harlem River. Originally 15 stone arches; the five mid river arches were replaced in 1926 to 1928 by one steel arch (about 400 to 450 ft). Surviving masonry: one arch on the Manhattan side (over the Harlem River Drive), ten on the Bronx side, which cross the Metro-North Hudson Line, the Major Deegan and Sedgwick Avenue. Herringbone brick walk, brick and granite parapets, Victorian lamps. The 200 ft octagonal Highbridge Water Tower (John B. Jervis, 1866 to 1872) stands at the Manhattan end in Highbridge Park. Reopened June 9, 2015.
