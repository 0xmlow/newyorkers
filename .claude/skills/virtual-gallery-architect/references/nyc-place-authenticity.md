# NYC place authenticity

## Reference-led place brief

Establish borough, neighborhood or address, building use and era, floor level, reference date, season, time of day, and intended fidelity. Choose only details that affect the requested scene; do not turn this into a questionnaire. For a fictional venue, identify it as fictional and use one coherent architectural family.

Build a small evidence ledger: feature / source URL or local file / source date / observed or inferred / dimension and confidence / modeling consequence. Prefer user photographs and measured plans for the actual interior, then official building or venue material and historic documentation. Historic sources establish historic appearance, not necessarily present conditions. Separate reference access from permission to redistribute source images or textures.

Useful primary starting points, checked September 2026:
- [LPC designation reports](https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/designations/designation-reports.page): architectural descriptions and historical context; follow the specific building or district report and record its page.
- [Discover NYC Landmarks](https://www.landmarks.nyc/): identify a property and its designation; a map footprint does not establish interior dimensions.
- [LPC](https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/): follow its designation photo collection for additional exterior evidence.

Use site-specific sources before claiming a building feature. Do not fabricate an address, skyline orientation, historic attribution, or measured dimension. Generated imagery is concept material, not site evidence.

## Typology choices, not neighborhood stereotypes

These are design starting points to verify against the chosen reference, not facts about every building in a district:

| Candidate venue | Features to investigate | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| SoHo cast-iron loft | Facade bay rhythm, column spacing, deep windows, structural floor and ceiling, street elevation | Pasting a cast-iron facade onto an unrelated interior |
| Chelsea industrial conversion | Original industrial shell, loading openings, service elements, gallery partitions, skylights if documented | Assuming every Chelsea gallery is a warehouse |
| Lower East Side storefront | Narrow frontage, threshold, street sightline, rear circulation, existing service core | Scaling a large museum down without changing proportions |
| Brooklyn warehouse conversion | Structural grid, masonry depth, loading history, roof structure, adjoining buildings | Generic brick plus an arbitrary Manhattan skyline |
| Townhouse or brownstone gallery | Stoop and entry level, room sequence, tall openings, party walls, floor transitions | Treating all floors as one open loft |
| Civic or landmark hall | Characteristic section, structural rhythm, ornament hierarchy, material junctions | Replacing recognizable structure with decorative props |

## Make the exterior support the interior

Resolve the view from the actual floor and window orientation. Model enough neighboring massing to explain occlusion, reflected light, street width, rooflines, and visible sky. In close views, give windows reveals, frames, sill depth, glazing, and believable wall thickness. A distant panorama may be sufficient for a restricted view; document it and inspect parallax during walking.

Add stoops, fire escapes, cornices, curbs, rooftop equipment, signage, street trees, or water tanks only where references or a plausible fictional brief support them. Avoid scattering recognizable NYC objects as substitutes for correct proportions. Prioritize the entrance, structural bays, window views, and room sequence over decorative density.

## Evidence checkpoint

Save a reference-aligned eye-level view, an entrance-to-gallery view, a reverse view toward the windows, and a close-up of one material junction as appropriate to scope. Compare framing, opening positions, column rhythm, wall thickness, and exterior occlusion before adding props. Record material deviations and unresolved geometry. For an MLow adaptation, keep the real architecture legible and concentrate the artistic intervention in curated art, installations, or deliberately identified additions.
