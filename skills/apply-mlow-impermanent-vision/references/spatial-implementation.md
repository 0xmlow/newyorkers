# MLow in navigable environments

For a code-based world, use the style as spatial and material behavior rather than a full-screen effect. Give each chamber a recognizable architectural substrate and a dominant transformation. Let quiet paths contrast with dense thresholds.

- Use object- or surface-space contour fields that bend with the modeled form. If static geometry is merged, verify which coordinate space the shader receives afterward.
- Make paint drips physical geometry where visitors can see silhouettes; reserve shader lines for fine structure. Explain whether depth comes from real geometry, displacement, bump, or a visual raymarch.
- Keep original artwork, posters, stickers, typography and logos unlit/neutral and aspect-correct. Put new paint behavior on architecture, not over source art.
- A sovereign Eye Flower can track the viewer or blink as an explicit action. Preserve the uncanny botanical form and a clear narrative purpose; do not multiply decorative flower icons to simulate density.
- Keep live portal views camera-dependent. Non-Euclidean topology, camera-reactive scale and bounded infinite-space shaders can express Melt without flattening every surface into rainbow noise.
- Respect reduced motion, provide ordinary controls for actions, and retain accessible artwork inspection. State when GPU visual validation is unavailable.

Use existing supplied asset packs and original posters where they carry authored detail. When delivering code for another builder, include the style references and runtime asset provenance with the source, while leaving originals untouched.

## Generated materials and portable scenes

When creating new environmental materials before a gallery build, distinguish generated color imagery from authored structural relief. Tile joints and metal corrugation can guide procedural height/normal maps, but colored paint luminance does not establish depth. Confirm alignment in grazing light before claiming a coherent PBR set. Use displacement only on sufficiently subdivided geometry and retain physical drip geometry where silhouette matters.

Keep a sovereign Eye Flower's behavior explicit: a camera-facing sculpture, scale-based blink and articulated eyelids are different implementations. Preserve the original authored model and document any runtime deformation. For code handoffs, ship exact style assets, map provenance, generation prompts and a truthful rendering-validation status.

## Learned on BIGGER ON THE INSIDE (2026-10-17)

- The contour field works best in world space with the thresholds as attractors: field = fbm(p * 0.22) * 3 + sum of
  exp(-d / r) over nearby doorways, lines every 1 / 9 to 1 / 16 of a unit, every fifth line heavier. Lines crowd and
  bend toward each door, so the paint reads as coming out of the threshold. Keep a coverage of 0 on facades (paint
  creeps from doors only) and 1 in the rooms that are Melt (the stairwell).
- Thin lines and a low glow (about 0.85 to 1.2) read as MLow; thick bright ones read as zebra stripes under bloom.
- Drips belong on casings and walls beside a doorway, never across it (a doorway is often a portal).
- The Eye Flower earns its place at scale: over the street at the far corner, and over the folded city on the roof,
  tracking the visitor, blinking when clicked. One per space.
- Pull the surface under the paint toward midnight navy (ground about 0.8) so the electric lines sit in a void.

## Material hierarchy from Unlicensed New York (2026-10-18)

Reserve the generated contour material for columns, portal jambs, and civic objects so it follows real volume; keep collection textures white and unlit. A generated inverse-depth skyline is an authored parallax estimate, not surveyed depth. Use it at a protected distant vista and label that provenance. Dense generated wheatpaste is an environmental surface, never a replacement for original collection works or exact brand marks.
