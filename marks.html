NEW YORK · ONE TALLY

5,555 left off the count. 150 get the mark.

Seen, made, numbered, and then not counted. Inside the release that carries the tally instead of the number.

When THE CENSUS RELEASE was cut from 6,666 to 1,111, five thousand five hundred and fifty five New Yorkers were left off the count. They had been seen. They had been made. They had been given a number. Then they were not counted.

THE MARKS are 150 of them, chosen across every era from II to XIX, all thirteen families and all five boroughs, with a floor under the Bronx and Staten Island.

A census taker counts in tally marks. Four strokes and a slash.

That is the whole idea. These New Yorkers carry the mark instead of the number, and after the mint the city keeps marking them: every hand a Mark passes through adds a stroke.

Eleven of them were THE ONES once. Counted first, sent back to the pool when KEYSTONE took that release's place, and left off again. They are the cast. Nine era closers, the last New Yorker made in their era, close the release at last call.

What makes a Mark different

Post no bills.

KEYSTONE grows by the artist's hand. THE CENSUS RELEASE changes on Fridays. A Mark changes by what happens to it. These are being built now, what ships is announced before the mint, and none of it is on chain yet.

No. 1

One seed.
No picks.

A shuffle seed is committed in public before the mint and mixed with a named future Ethereum block. Nobody, the artist included, decides who gets which.

No. 2 · Have you seen this New Yorker?

Every hand adds a stroke.

Mint, sale or gift, each one chalks a stroke into the corner. The fifth opens THE MARKED. Tear a tab.

    No. 3 · The city's clock

    Day, night, rain.

    The token reads New York time and weather and shows the state the city is in right now.

    No. 4 · Service change

    Hold a borough, draw a line.

    Marks held together in one borough draw a line between them. Complete it and every Mark on it opens a state.

    The fair shuffle

    This is not three card monte.

    Nobody picks.
    Not even MLow.

    1. Before the mintThe list of 150 and the hash of a secret seed are published here. The list is already fixed, its hash is on the receipt.
    2. After the mint closesThe seed is revealed and mixed with the hash of an Ethereum block named in advance, a block nobody can know yet.
    3. The revealPublic code shuffles the 150 with that number. Anyone can run it and get the same answer.
