The short version

What is NEW YORKERS?

NEW YORKERS is a painted census of New York City by the artist MLow. He has painted 7,541 characters so far, across 19 chapters he calls eras, and he is still painting. Every one of them is a New Yorker: the heroes, the strangers, the animals, the villains. Each gets a number, a name, a story and a place on the city map. All of it is published free on this site, and parts of it are sold in named releases.

Who made it?

MLow, an artist born in New York City who works with AI. He has lived here 27 years and paints from a personal photography archive built over those years. He trains his own AI models on that archive, generates at volume, then finishes every piece by hand in Photoshop. His art has run on more than 5,000 New York City taxis and has been shown in 50 group exhibitions with Art Crush Gallery across 16 countries in 2026.

Why should I care?

Because the work is about being seen. Eight million people share this city and most of them go uncounted. NEW YORKERS gives a portrait to the ones a census would only tally: the bodega cat, the night nurse, the landlord, the kid off the bus with two suitcases. If you have ever lived here, somebody in it is you.

Is it for sale?

Parts of it are, in named releases, never all at once. Two are open right now. KEYSTONE is the founding New Yorkers as dynamic ones of one on Transient Labs, indexed on SuperRare, 111 and counting at 0.069 ETH each. THE CENSUS RELEASE is 6,666 tokens on OpenSea at 0.0042 ETH, open to everyone, from 23 September at 11:11 AM ET until 23 October at 11:11 AM ET. THE MARKS is 90 pieces, each one unique, on SuperRare and Transient Labs, in waves announced one at a time. Nothing is ever added to a release once it closes, and no New Yorker is ever sold twice.

The idea and the why

Why did MLow make NEW YORKERS?

He made it to count the people the city forgets to count. The work draws on 27 years of living here and on a photography archive he built the whole time, so the streets behind the characters are streets he stood on. He painted through cancer, and the eye flower, the flower with a human iris that recurs in the art, came out of that. Chai Lifeline, the project's charity partner, supported him through treatment. One question holds every piece together: what does it mean to be seen in a city of eight million.

What does the eye flower mean?

The eye flower is a flower with a human iris, and it stands for the city's attention. It blooms in a frame where a moment was truly witnessed. The iris is human because the city's eyes are its people. A flower open beside a character means somebody was watching, and watching with love.

What does the blue evil eye mean?

The blue evil eye is protection. It watches so you can stop watching your back. In the stills it never moves and never blinks. It is also MLow's sovereign mark, the signature that runs through every collection he makes.

What is the Blossom sigil?

The circle grid flower sigil is community. It is the Blossom mark, and it appears where people have built something together. In the lore a figure called the Sigil Writer paints them at 4am and has never been seen. Blossom itself is the wider ecosystem around MLow's work rather than a collection with a piece count.

What does "the city is watching back" mean?

It means the census runs both ways. Every census counts people. This one looks back at them. The eyes in the art are the city's eyes, and the city's eyes are its people: the neighbor at the window, the driver who waits, the stranger who helps you up the subway stairs. THE CITY IS WATCHING BACK is the campaign line, and it is a promise rather than a threat.

Why are villains in a love letter to New York?

Because a city is not only its heroes. The landlord, the broker fee, the machine and the money shaped New York as much as anyone who saved it. Villains here are painted with the same care as everyone else and played with style rather than cruelty. Some earn a redemption in a later era. The one line for the whole project says it: every New Yorker gets a portrait, even the villains.

How the collection is built

What is an era, and how many are there?

An era is a chapter of the census, a run of numbers painted under one idea. There are 19 so far. They open with The Originals at number 1 and run to The Trait Layer, which ends at 7,990. Each carries a title: The Eye Flower Era, The Masterworks, The Mythos, The Deep City, The Twenty Houses, The Ten Rooms, The MLow Show. Characters carry forward, so a face from one era can return in a later one, and more eras are still being painted.

What are the families?

Thirteen, and each one is a tribe rather than a category. They are Ticker Tape, The Machine, Corner Hustle, Alley Cats, Third Rail, Front Steps, Hard Hats, After Hours, Two Suitcases, Eight Million, The Blocks, Made Here, Open Notebook. Every character belongs to exactly one. The family is part of what the piece is, so it travels with the work as a trait.

What are the four axes?

Every character holds an address on four axes: borough, time band, family and register. Borough is identity rather than location: Manhattan the stage, Brooklyn the soul, Queens the world in one borough, the Bronx the origin, Staten Island the ferry. The time band is the city's emotional clock: morning, afternoon, golden hour, witching hour. The register is the key the portrait is played in, from reverent to comic to menacing to tender. Name all four and you have the character's address in the census.

What is a set, and how are sets categorised?

A set is a named group of characters covering a block of numbers, and there are 160. They come from two places. Some were designed as collections in their own right: The Hours, The Months, The Moons, The Seasons, The Elements, The Decades, The Subway Lines, The Bridges, The Lost Buildings, The New York Tarot. The rest come from the eras themselves, where each era's houses and arcs become sets automatically. Sets are the quests of the collection: own your hour, own your line, own your month.

What is a monument?

A monument is a milestone number, a piece that carries a turn in the story. Every hundred mark is one, so 100, 200, 300 and every hundred after it. So is the closing number of every era, the era openers, and the lead characters of the houses and rooms in the later eras. The census marks 204 of them. THE MARKS, the 90 ones of one, are drawn from part of that list.

What are traits and how do they work?

Traits are objects a character wears, and they live in one era: Era XIX, The Trait Layer, at numbers 7491 to 7990. There are 31 of them: hoodie, beanie, gold chain, 3D glasses, pit vipers, eye patch, clown nose, laser eyes, halo, crown, third eye and more. Each is painted as a real object in the frame rather than pasted on as a layer. A weighted script dealt the exact set across the 500 numbers, so the published rarity table is a count and not an estimate. Counts run from 43 hoodies down to 2 third eyes.

What is a state?

A state is one finished version of a character. The same New Yorker can exist as Night Street, the default house style, and as Stencil, Saturday Morning, Dream State, Motion or Witching Hour. Witching Hour is earned in person and is never shown online. Every character has at least one finished state, and 97 carry more than one today. A dynamic token holds a stack of states: the artist adds new ones over time and the owner picks which one the token shows.

What is a glitch edition?

A glitch edition is a corrupted loop of a piece, made with the treatment from MLow's STILL WAITING collection. The still is broken into an endlessly looping CRT and VHS collapse: the signal degrades, the city persists. 699 pieces carry one, in the numbers 767 to 1466. On the site they load only when you pick them.

The art itself

How is the art actually made?

MLow trains custom AI models on his own photography, generates at volume, then finishes every piece by hand in Photoshop. He calls the method digital alchemy. The generation is raw material and the selection is the work: most of what comes out never becomes a piece. Every record in the census shows the prompt it came from, under FROM THE STUDIO.

What does a custom model mean?

It means the model was trained on MLow's own pictures rather than used off the shelf. 27 years of New York photography is the training material, so the light, the wet asphalt and the street furniture come from frames he shot himself. A stock model gives you a generic city. This one gives you his.

What is the open notebook?

The open notebook is the project's promise to show its work. The prompts, the successive versions of the house style, the refires, the cost ledgers and the pieces that were cut are all published. Most AI work hides what it threw away. This one publishes it, because the choosing is the work.

What gets cut, and why?

Work gets cut when it fails the standard, and the cuts stay published rather than buried. A piece is refired when the anatomy breaks, when the style drifts, or when stray lettering appears in the frame, which a text check catches across every era. One finished piece has been rejected outright in curation, and its record is still on the site. The much larger cut happens earlier, among the generations that never became pieces at all.

How would you describe the style?

Glossy vinyl toy figures with clean white sticker outlines, set down in a photoreal New York night. Neon on wet asphalt, steam off the grate, a bridge behind. The blue is the evil eye blue, a mark of protection rather than a wash. Everyone gets the toy treatment, which is why even the villains read as affectionate.

Where it lives

What is the Atlas and how is each piece placed?

The Atlas is the map of the census, and it puts the pieces on the real city. 7,307 of the 7,541 are placed. Placement comes from the work itself: the borough field, the title, the story and the prompt, then a curated read where a person looked at the scene and named the block. Some pins are anchored to a known address and some are read from the scene, and the map marks which is which.

What is the museum?

The museum is a walkable gallery of 111 New York rooms, built in the browser. The Bowery after hours, the Guggenheim ramp, the Rose Reading Room, the crown of the Statue, Studio 8H, the whale at Natural History. Each room hangs the characters the census recorded at that place first, then any era, family or set you choose. The light follows the real New York clock and the hang reshuffles daily. No room is a scan or a replica: every one is an interpretation.

What is the reading room?

The reading room is the history layer of the site. It holds 20 sourced articles about the real city, including the subway of 1904, the consolidation of 1898, the blackout of 1977 and the birth of hip hop, plus a history page for each of the 111 museum rooms. Every fact is tied to an institution you can check. The census paints the city and the reading room explains it.

What do the numbers mean?

The number is a character's line in the census, the way a real census gives every person a line. Numbering runs from 1 to 7,990, because the eras painted so far reserve that many lines. Not every line is painted yet, and most of the open ones sit in Era I, the oldest era and the least filled. Numbers carry structure too: eras are runs of numbers, sets are runs of numbers, and every hundredth number is a monument.

Getting counted and the release

What is THE COUNT?

THE COUNT is the census taken in person. The Census Office at NFT.NYC is where you answer the questions, get counted, and leave with the New Yorker the city matched you with, held in your name on a paper Resident Card. It costs nothing and needs no wallet. The number of people counted is not public, and it will not be estimated.

What is the difference between KEYSTONE and THE CENSUS RELEASE?

They are two drops on two contracts, and they are not the same work. KEYSTONE is the founding New Yorkers as ones of one: 111 and counting, 0.069 ETH each, minted straight onto Transient Labs ERC-7160 and indexed on SuperRare, so the art inside a piece keeps growing after you own it and you pin the state that shows. It is open ended and grows as new Keystone New Yorkers are painted. THE CENSUS RELEASE is the census proper: 6,666 pieces at 0.0042 ETH on OpenSea, one public window from 23 September to 23 October, minted as a standard token that can be evolved into the same dynamic form afterwards. KEYSTONE is the spine of the city. The census is the city.

What is THE CLAIM?

THE CLAIM is the day the character held in your name becomes yours onchain, the mint. It is open now. THE CENSUS RELEASE mints on OpenSea from Wednesday 23 September 2026 at 11:11 AM ET until Friday 23 October 2026 at 11:11 AM ET, at 0.0042 ETH, with no list and no wallet requirements.

How do I get counted?

Answer six questions on the GET COUNTED page. The city matches you with a character, you can download a Resident card, and you can register so the match is held in your name for THE CLAIM. No wallet is needed. You can also get counted in person at the Census Office.

What is the quiz?

The quiz is six questions and no wallet. It asks what you do with a locked door, who you are when the train stops between stations, which hour belongs to you, what deserves protection, who your witness is, and which sentence sounds like home. Your answers weight you toward a family, and the city hands you a character from it. Era I is left out of the pool, because it holds named real people.

How do I nominate a New Yorker?

Use the nomination form on the GET COUNTED page. You give a name or an alias for the credit, the memory in your own words, an optional photo and your contact, then tick separate boxes: one saying the memory is yours to send, one to publish the story, one to allow it to be adapted into artwork. One nomination is picked every Sunday, painted on Monday and posted on Tuesday at 11:11 AM. The nominator is credited by alias and the subject is never tagged. The painting comes back to the nominator as a gift, and a gift piece is never sold and never used in an advertisement.

Is there an allowlist?

No. THE CENSUS RELEASE is open to everyone, minting now on OpenSea until 23 October at 11:11 AM ET, so there is nothing to get on and no list to be late for. Getting counted is a separate thing: free, no wallet, and it holds a character in your name.

What will it cost?

0.0042 ETH a piece in THE CENSUS RELEASE, 6,666 of them, on OpenSea, about $11 when it opened. The price is set in ETH, so its value in dollars moves with ETH. The public window opened on 23 September 2026 at 11:11 AM ET and runs until 23 October at 11:11 AM ET. Prices for other releases are published before each one opens and never move afterwards.

Owning it

What do I actually own?

You own a character, not one picture of them. The Keystone New Yorkers are ones of one on an ERC-7160 contract: the token holds a stack of finished versions, called states, the artist appends new ones over time, nothing already made is deleted, and you decide which one shows. A census New Yorker mints on OpenSea as a standard token and can be evolved into that same kind of dynamic token afterwards. Either way you own the character. Unique does not mean finished.

What is ERC-7160, and what does pinning mean?

ERC-7160 is a token standard for art where one token can hold more than one image: the token holds a stack of finished states of a character, the artist appends new ones over time, and the owner picks which one shows. Picking is called pinning, and you can change your pick. The standard comes from Transient Labs. The Keystone New Yorkers are dynamic ones of one on Transient Labs ERC-7160, indexed on SuperRare. The artist appends variants and new art over time, and the owner pins the one that shows. THE CENSUS RELEASE mints on OpenSea as a standard token. A census New Yorker can then be evolved into a dynamic ERC-7160 New Yorker holding several variants, and the artist keeps adding to it.

If I buy a print, do I own one of the collection?

No. A print is not a token and it is not a New Yorker. Prints are a separate thing entirely, and MLow's print catalogue comes from different bodies of work. Buying a print gives you the print and nothing in this collection.

What rights come with a piece?

Rights are set out in writing rather than assumed. Contributors keep their own material: nominating a New Yorker hands over neither your story nor your photograph, and commercial use of a story or a likeness is a separate signed agreement, never a checkbox. Collector terms are not final. Era I is excluded from the sale, from advertising and from the quiz, because it holds named real people and licensed characters.

Which charity does the project support?

Chai Lifeline is the charity partner. They supported MLow through cancer treatment, which is where the eye flowers came from. He has raised more than $250,000 for children's cancer causes through Art Basel Miami auctions. The exact share of proceeds is not published yet.

Will there be more releases after these two?

Yes, and that is the plan from the start. The census is still being painted, new eras are still coming, and one nomination is chosen and painted every week. When a new release is announced it will say exactly what is in it, how many, in what format, on what platform and when. Nothing is ever quietly added to a release that has already closed.

If more will be released later, what stops it being endless?

Two things, and neither is a cap on the total. No New Yorker is ever sold twice, so nothing you own is reissued or diluted. And no release is ever reopened or topped up, so the release you bought into stays the size it was when you bought into it. Growth happens by adding new named releases in the open, not by quietly enlarging old ones.

What happens to work that is not in a release?

It stays published free on this site and it is not for sale yet. It is not being withheld forever either. It is simply not its turn. Every character keeps its number, its story and its place on the atlas whether it has been released or not.

MLow and the wider work

Where has this work actually been seen?

MLow's work has run on more than 5,000 New York City taxis and has been shown in 50 group exhibitions with Art Crush Gallery across 16 countries in 2026. NEW YORKERS itself lives here: every character is published free on this site, on the atlas and in the museum, whether or not it is in a release.

How does NEW YORKERS fit with MLow's other collections?

NEW YORKERS is the largest body of work he has made and the most local. THE SOFT CONSPIRACY, a collaboration with the painter Andrés Del Vecchio, is the secret world of operatives and dossiers. fLOWers is roughly 1,800 collage flowers made after surviving cancer, and the eye flowers here grew out of them. Transdimensional Trippers paints the self in struggle and recovery. Impermanent Loss paints flux as abstraction. Tokenized Garbage keeps what the city throws away. STILL WAITING is the wait as a permanent condition, and its glitch treatment produced the 699 glitch editions in this collection. Blossom is the ecosystem around all of it rather than a collection with a count. The throughline across every one is Tripping and Rising.

Where can I buy a print?

Prints are sold through MLow's own shop, reached at MLow.nyc. The catalogue holds 235 works from four bodies of work: Transdimensional Trippers, fLOWers, Impermanent Loss and Tokenized Garbage, with prices starting at $220. Editions are signed, numbered, capped, and retired when they sell out. None of them are NEW YORKERS.
