vangogh_c: MoMA, November 1935, Vincent van Gogh, the first blockbuster (huge crowds), or the Bedroom painting with its skewed walls. Trick: the hall is an Ames room built on the Bedroom's perspective, so walking from one corner to the other you grow and shrink against the pieces; or the brush strokes flow out of the frames over the walls as you walk.
picasso_c: MoMA, November 1939, Picasso: Forty Years of His Art, the show that brought Guernica to New York; or the 1980 MoMA retrospective that took the whole museum. Trick: a faceted cubist hall where each piece hangs on hinged panels that show it from three angles at once, and the hall folds as you move.
banksy_c: Housing Works thrift shop, Eighth Avenue, October 2013, where he donated a thrift store landscape he had painted a Nazi officer into, The Banality of the Banality of Evil, later sold at auction for 615,000 dollars. The hall is the thrift shop: MLow's pieces hang in mismatched secondhand frames with handwritten price tags among lamps and racks. Trick: price tags change when you are not looking; one painting in the shop has a stencil figure added only when seen through your phone (a screen in the room).
