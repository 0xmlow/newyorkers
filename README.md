# ON LOCATION: New York at the Movies

A walkable three.js gallery by MLow. A hundred and five scenes New York gave the movies, rebuilt as sets and recast with
NEW YORKERS: every member of the cast has an eye flower for a head, so the scenes are the films' and the people
are ours (no actor's likeness anywhere, no studio logo; the no ghost sign is an eye flower behind the bar).
Each set's walls hold twelve census New Yorkers from the block where the film was shot (`HOODS` in build.py).

Open it: double click `Open ON LOCATION.command`. On the site it is `movies.html`
(`NEW YORKERS SITE/_build/build_galleries.py` copies `site/` into `assets/movies`).

## Round twelve: CC0 props made ours, every set dressed (2026-10-10)

MLow asked for CC0 props tied to each film, edited in Blender to be NEW YORKERS and MLow, in every set, more of them, and
extremely impressive. `AUDIT_2026-10-10.md` measured the gallery first: sets 16 to 105 averaged 3.2 props, eleven had
none, and not one of the 116 props carried the brand. `UPGRADE_BRIEF_2026-10-10.md` set the bar each set had to meet.

- **The Blender brand pass.** `_build/brand_pass.py` takes a Poly Haven model and repaints its colour textures in the
  palette (a gradient map that keeps the wear, a tint, or gold), presses stickers onto the real surface (a ray finds the
  face, a 9 by 9 grid wraps onto the mesh 2 mm proud) and adds nazar discs and banners. `_build/brand.py` draws any text
  label with PIL and runs one Blender per recipe. Stickers are `_build/stickers.py`: eye flowers, nazars, six N3W logos,
  MLow tags, COUNTED stamps, New York plates and labels. Recipes: `_build/brand/*.json` (577 of them).
- **The library.** 210 Poly Haven models now ship (CC0), and 577 NEW YORKERS edits of them: a shared street kit
  (`common.json`: DSNY green bins of the DEPT. OF THE COUNTED, pink eye flower hydrants, N3W BELL payphones, banner lamp
  posts, the N.Y. COUNTED DEPT. barrier) and a set of edits per film (`b1.json` to `b8.json`, named `nyb_<set>_<thing>`).
- **Every set** gained eight or more objects, at least three of them made for that film, a hero edit of its signature
  object (Big's gilded chess set, Wall Street's gold bull, Gatsby's gilt spectacles, the 12 Angry Men fan that only spins
  at the verdict, Kong's EIGHTH WONDER crate), swaps of its stock props for branded ones, walls and ceilings dressed,
  and one or more new eggs. 455 eggs in all.
- **How it is wired.** Each batch file `_build/src/up/b1.js` to `b8.js` owns its sets: `PROPS` rows, `ROOM` code (with a
  kit of build.js helpers) and `SWAP`, which turns a stock prop into its edit in one set. Eight builders worked at once
  with `_build/devsite.py` copies, `build.py --out` and `shot.py --http`. `build.py` now ships only models some set names.
- **Fixed on the way:** stock baseball bats loading metres tall, the Lost Weekend table at 1.8 m, the Katz tables at bench
  height, a wall lantern standing in for street lamps in three sets, the library asking for a model never downloaded, an
  oil drum in 1863, the Odd Couple chips hidden inside their table.
- **Brand check:** the Odd Couple wall had a real Yankees pennant, Knicks and Giants posters and photos with real looking
  faces; the Llewyn wall had a Bob Dylan album and a Joan Baez poster. Painted over (`_build/paint_walls_r12.py`).
- **Not verified:** most of the new eggs were never clicked by hand (a few were clicked by script); nothing was tried on a
  phone; model weight per set rose (the package's models went from 119 MB to about 460 MB, each set loading only its own
  and its neighbours'), and frame rate on a weak laptop is unmeasured. Spend: nothing (CC0 models, Blender, no generation).

## Props: Poly Haven, CC0

116 props are Poly Haven models (polyhaven.com, CC0): street lamps, hydrants, trash cans, manholes, barriers,
crates, chandeliers, sofas, chairs, desks, a cash register, a coffee cart, binoculars, cameras, a projector,
clocks, statues and more. `python3 _build/polyhaven.py id[:tris] ...` downloads the 1k glTF, runs the
museum's prop pass (`_build/ph_pass.py`, prop_pass reading glTF) at 512 px textures into
`work/glb_pass/ph_<id>.glb` and deletes the download. `PROPS` in build.js places them per set; every model gets
a soft contact shadow. brass_pan_01, brass_pot_01, CheeseBox_01, garden_gnome and concrete_cat_statue do not
pass (Blender produces nothing), nor do fir_tree_01 and pine_tree_01, so the Copa's pots and the Rockefeller tree are drawn in code. polyhaven.py runs one asset per Blender call, and a dropped download skips that asset instead of the batch.

## Brand check

Round eight (sets 65 to 74) was made in Adobe Firefly (Gemini 3.1, Nano Banana 2) because the Krea balance ran out: plates at 21:9 and walls at 16:9, 2K, driven from the browser, and the cast reuses Tripo models already in the gallery (no new 3D). The Devil Wears Prada plate had two magazine covers with human faces; eye flowers were painted over them.

## How it was made

1. Plates: Nano Banana Pro on Krea, 21:9 2K, eye level, one point perspective, with the eye flower, the N3W
   logo and a Mark as references (`work/jobs.json`). `python3 _build/fetch.py` downloads finished jobs.
2. `python3 _build/depth.py work/plates_raw/*.jpg` (Depth Anything V2 Small, local) writes plates and depth.
3. Walls: Nano Banana Pro flat elevations, `python3 _build/walls.py`.
4. Cast and props: Nano Banana Pro concepts on white (`work/concepts`), Tripo H3.1 on Krea
   (`work/glb_urls.txt`), the museum's `blender/prop_pass.py` into `work/glb_pass`, facing read off
   `_build/front_sheet.py` renders into `YAW` in main.js. Hydrant, pigeon and rat are THE MARKS heroes.
5. `python3 _build/build.py` (or `--js`), then `python3 _build/shot.py <dir> name=room=natm ...` for headless
   screenshots (it deletes its Chrome profile afterwards: each one is 50 to 100 MB).

## Round thirteen (2026-10-11): phones, the finishing pass, a new illusion in every set

- **Sets build lazily.** `main.js` builds a set when you come within one (phone) or two (desktop) of it, and frees the
  GPU copies of sets more than two (phone) or five away; on a phone their paintings, plates and wallpapers are dropped
  and fetched again on return. So anything a set makes must live in its group `g`. Before this a phone fetched 1,607
  files (302 MB) at load and gave up; now 92 files, 26 MB.
- **The finishing pass runs everywhere.** The scene renders into a target flagged like an XR target, so tone mapping
  happens inside it and the plates are untouched; the pass adds bloom, vignette, fringing and grain. A set tunes it with
  `r.post = { bloom, vig, ca, sat, heat, grain }`. `?lite` turns it off.
- **Phones get a SETS button** instead of the 105 button rail.
- `src/up/c1.js` to `c8.js`: one new illusion, graphics and one egg per set (see `ROUND13_BRIEF.md`). 565 eggs.
- Trap: headless shots fill Chrome's Metal shader cache (`$TMPDIR/../C/com.google.Chrome.helper/com.apple.metal`,
  1.3 GB in an hour of eight builders). It is a cache; clear it when the disk is short.

## Skate mode (2026-10-11)

SKATE THE SETS on the opening card (or `?skate` in the address) starts the film on a board; the SKATE pill at the
bottom left (it carries your goals found of 315), K on a keyboard, or Y (View) on a gamepad switch in any set. W or the right trigger pushes, S or the left
trigger brakes, A and D or the stick steer, SPACE or A ollies, 1 2 3 or X B Y flip the board in the air (OLLIE on a
phone; tap it again in the air for a flip). Ollie onto a rail or a ledge edge to grind it. A combo scores only on a
clean landing; a wall or an unfinished flip is a bail. Backquote or `?debug` adds a line with room, speed, air, y and
how many spots the set has. `?overview` puts the camera above the set to check its spots.

  up them, rails lock on when you come down onto them roughly along their line, gaps pay when you take off in one
  circle and land in the other. `node _build/src/skate.test.mjs` runs eleven checks.

Walk audit, the way out, and THE WRAP (2026-10-12)
--------------------------------------------------
MLow could not get to the other side of the Pelham car, and said a few other sets were unclear about the next room.
- `_build/audit_walk.py`: every set loaded headless (a fresh Chrome per six), the floor flood filled on a 10 cm grid
  from the arrival point and from both doors with the engine's own colliders (`window.__colliders`, radius 0.32 like
  `collide()`) and the skate surfaces (a step up over 0.3 m is refused, like the walk). Prints, per set, whether the
  arrival point reaches each door and the doors reach each other, the narrowest squeeze on the widest route, and the
  cells that cause it. Exit 1 when a door is unreachable or a route is under 1.0 m. Run it after touching any set:
  `python3 _build/audit_walk.py` (all 105), `--rooms pelham,wrap` for a few. A collider marked `timed` (the Uncut
  Gems buzzer) is treated as a door.
- What it found: Pelham's car had its doorway cut one panel south of the gap in its collider (the open door hit a
  wall, a shut panel let you through), a bench across the doorway, no door on the east side and an invisible wall
  at the south end; the arrival point (10, 5.8) had a prop parked on it in Saturday Night Fever, Stuart Little, King
  Kong and The French Connection, and Tiffany's had an onlooker boxing it in; the Uncut Gems buzzer only buzzed from
  the airlock side, so you could never leave west. All fixed in place. `go()` and `enter()` now `settle()` you out of
  anything you land in, so a prop on the arrival point can never trap you again.
- `N`, or the WAY OUT pill: a dotted line on the floor from your feet to the next set's door, searched on the live
  colliders and skate surfaces (`wayFind` in main.js), recomputed as you walk. If there is no route it says so and
  names the `]` jump. After ninety seconds in one set without it, one toast mentions it, once per visit.
- THE WRAP (`src/up/e1.js`, set 105, the last door before Kong): an escape room on Stage 4 at Astoria, from
  `ESCAPE_ROOM_RESEARCH_2026-10-12.md` (sixteen NYC venues, the conventions, what players hate). Sixty minutes on the
  clock, six puzzles: the four slates in call time order across midnight open the prop cage (4 digits); the UV torch
  from the cage lights six marks on the one sheet, the directional lock on the flight case takes them and gives the
  MetroCard; the lighting board set to the office chart pops locker 3 for the brass key; the bodega shelves counted
  through the key card spell the word; the stage door (the east door) wants word, card and key, in any order. A game
  master monitor gives a hint a click (counted), the walkie nudges after five quiet minutes, the red bar by the west
  door is the emergency exit and always works. Before the clap and after the escape, both doors are ordinary doors.
  Best time in `onloc.wrap`. `work/wrap_test.js` drives the whole room headless through `shot.py --eval`.
